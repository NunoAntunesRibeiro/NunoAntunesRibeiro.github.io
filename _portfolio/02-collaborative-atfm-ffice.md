## Research Problem

Air Traffic Flow Management (ATFM) aims to balance traffic demand with available airport and airspace capacity before congestion results in excessive airborne delay and holding.

This problem is particularly challenging in **Southeast Asia**, where flights frequently cross multiple Flight Information Regions (FIRs) managed by different countries and organizations. Unlike highly centralized airspace systems, ATFM decisions may therefore be made with only local information and limited coordination.

The introduction of **Flight and Flow Information for a Collaborative Environment (FF-ICE/R1)** creates new opportunities for information sharing and collaborative decision-making across FIR boundaries.

## Contributions

Our research develops **optimization-based simulation frameworks** to quantify the value of information sharing and collaborative ATFM in decentralized airspace networks.

Key contributions include:

- Rolling-horizon simulation of **decentralized ATFM operations across Southeast Asia**
- Optimization models that account for airport and airspace capacity, aircraft separation, speed adjustments, target times over waypoints, and airborne holding
- Explicit representation of different levels of **information availability and coordination between FIRs**
- Simulation of FF-ICE/R1-enabled collaboration and **Calculated Take-Off Time (CTOT)** assignment
- Quantification of how collaboration can shift delay from the air to the ground, reducing airborne time and fuel consumption
- Evaluation of **partial versus region-wide collaboration**, supporting phased implementation strategies for Southeast Asia
